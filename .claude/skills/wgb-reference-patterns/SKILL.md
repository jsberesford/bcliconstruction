# WGB Reference Patterns

Use this skill any time a section of the BCLI home page (or a comparable landing section) is being designed or coded. These patterns are the shared visual vocabulary the site is built on.

## Page structure

- **Long-scroll, full-width sections.** Each section spans the full viewport width and stacks vertically. No horizontal-scroll storytelling, no carousel-first sections.
- **Generous whitespace between sections.** Vertical breathing room is part of the design, not wasted space. Aim for section padding that feels almost too generous, then keep it.
- **Subtle scroll reveals on every section heading.** A small upward translate plus opacity fade as the heading enters the viewport, governed by the motion-discipline rules. Body content can follow with a short stagger.

## Headlines

- **Sentence case, not all caps.** A confident sentence reads more grown-up than a shouted slogan.
- Headlines are declarative, not interrogative. They state, they do not ask.
- One section H2 per section, with a single yellow underline accent on one or two words of that H2 (see bcli-color-discipline).

## Section patterns available

The following patterns are part of the vocabulary. Pick the one that fits the section's job. Do not invent new patterns when one of these already fits.

### Pain-points-as-cards (used for BCLI services)

A grid of cards, each card naming a specific problem the visitor recognizes ("delays from poor site planning," "rented equipment that arrives broken," etc.), with a one-line statement of how BCLI addresses it. Cards are flat, cream or beige background, ink text, gray supporting text. No yellow card fills.

### Comparison block (optional for BCLI)

A two-column or table-style block contrasting "the usual" against "with BCLI." Use sparingly; only when there is a concrete, defensible contrast.

### Founder photo blocks at the closing CTA

The closing CTA is anchored by founder photography (Leta, and any co-principal). Photo on one side, statement on the other. This is where the human face of the company appears.

### Two-column layouts

A confident statement on one side, supporting detail on the other. Use this for service intros, the founding story, and the methodology section. Keep the statement short; let the supporting column carry the detail.

## Decorative line illustrations

- The reference uses orbital decorative SVGs. **Reimagine these as construction line illustrations for BCLI**: site plans, level lines, plumb lines, surveyor cross-hairs, structural sections, equipment silhouettes drawn in single-weight line work.
- Line illustrations carry **one** yellow accent stroke each (per bcli-color-discipline). The rest of the lines are ink at reduced opacity or gray.
- Illustrations are decorative, not informational. They live in the margins and behind type, never as required reading.

## Before finalizing a section

Check: is this section using one of the named patterns? Does the headline follow sentence case with one underlined accent? Is the whitespace generous? Is there a scroll reveal on the heading? If any answer is no, fix it before moving on.
